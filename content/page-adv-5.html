The Hidden Problem: Low-Grade Parasites and the Eggs You Never See

[VIDEO]

Dogs pick up intestinal parasites from everyday life — puddles, parks, sniffing other dogs’ poop. Many puppies are even born with roundworms passed on from their mother.

First, parasites multiply quietly. A single female roundworm can lay up to around 200,000 eggs a day — each one far too small to see in your dog’s stool.

Second, they can slip past a single test. Some parasites, like whipworms and Giardia, shed eggs on and off, so a stool sample taken on a “quiet” day can come back clear. These low-grade infections are easy to miss.

And exposure is everywhere: a 2020 study of U.S. dog parks (the DOGPARCS study) found intestinal parasites at 85% of the parks tested [1], and national testing data showed canine hookworm cases rose by 47% between 2012 and 2018 [2].

Third, they can unsettle the whole gut. When the gut isn’t comfortable, it often shows up in other places:

  • The paw licking that won’t stop.
  • The scooting.
  • Loose stools or the occasional vomit.
  • A dull, itchy coat.
  • Low energy and restless nights.

Your dog doesn’t need to pass visible worms to have a gut that needs support. These signs can have other causes too — so if they persist, check in with your vet.
